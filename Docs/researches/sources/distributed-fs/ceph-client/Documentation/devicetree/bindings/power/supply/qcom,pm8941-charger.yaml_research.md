# sources/distributed-fs/ceph-client/Documentation/devicetree/bindings/power/supply/qcom,pm8941-charger.yaml

## Purpose
`sources/distributed-fs/ceph-client/Documentation/devicetree/bindings/power/supply/qcom,pm8941-charger.yaml` defines the Linux Devicetree YAML schema for `Qualcomm Switch-Mode Battery Charger and Boost`, a battery, fuel-gauge, or battery-monitor binding. It is declarative ABI documentation and validation input, not executable driver code. Its job is to keep DTS/DTB nodes aligned with the resources, child nodes, and electrical or timing constraints expected by the matching kernel subsystem before a driver probes hardware.

## Important APIs, Types, and Functions
The API surface is the schema contract: `$id`, `$schema`, `title`, `maintainers`, `properties`, `patternProperties`, `required`, composition keys, examples, and referenced common schemas. `compatible` uses an `enum` with 2 tokens: `qcom,pm8226-charger`, `qcom,pm8941-charger`. Top-level properties are `compatible`, `reg`, `interrupts`, `interrupt-names`, `qcom,fast-charge-current-limit`, `qcom,fast-charge-low-threshold-voltage`, `qcom,fast-charge-high-threshold-voltage`, `qcom,fast-charge-safe-voltage`, `qcom,fast-charge-safe-current`, `qcom,auto-recharge-threshold-voltage`, `qcom,minimum-input-voltage`, `qcom,dc-current-limit`, `qcom,disable-dc`, `qcom,jeita-extended-temp-range`, `usb-charge-current-limit`, `usb-otg-in-supply`, `otg-vbus`. Top-level required properties are `compatible`, `reg`, `interrupts`, `interrupt-names`; required properties collected from nested branches include `compatible`, `interrupt-names`, `interrupts`, `reg`. Patterned child schemas are none. Numeric and collection constraints include `maxItems=1`, `minimum=100000`, `maximum=3000000`, `minimum=2100000`, `maximum=3600000`, `minimum=3240000`, `maximum=5000000`, `minimum=4200000`, `maximum=9600000`, `maximum=2500000`. Important resource roles: `compatible` is the stable hardware ABI and OF match key; `reg` gives MMIO, I2C, SPI, child address, or selector placement; `interrupts` routes alert, status, protection, capture, timer, or controller IRQs; `interrupt-names` routes alert, status, protection, capture, timer, or controller IRQs; `qcom,fast-charge-current-limit` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,fast-charge-low-threshold-voltage` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,fast-charge-high-threshold-voltage` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,fast-charge-safe-voltage` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,fast-charge-safe-current` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,auto-recharge-threshold-voltage` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,minimum-input-voltage` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `qcom,dc-current-limit` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `usb-charge-current-limit` constrains electrical limits or measurement calibration; `usb-otg-in-supply` links board power rails through the regulator framework. The highest-risk ABI details are compatible identity, I2C/MMIO address, battery phandle, monitor resistor/calibration values, alert/IRQ wiring, charge/voltage/current limits, and vendor battery profile data.

## Control Flow
Control flow is dt-schema evaluation rather than imperative execution. `dt_binding_check` loads the core meta-schema, resolves `$ref` entries, applies `properties` and `patternProperties`, evaluates no top-level conditionals, checks required fields and min/max constraints, validates examples, and enforces the closure policy. At runtime this YAML does not execute: the compiled DTB node is matched through a kernel OF table or subsystem-specific child-node parser, then helpers for power-supply, regulator, PWM, PTP, PPS, PowerPC platform, clocks, resets, interrupts, GPIOs, pinctrl, power domains, and MFD parents consume the validated resources.

## State and Persistence Behavior
The file stores no mutable runtime state and writes no persistent data. Its persistence is ABI stability: compatible strings, required resource names, phandle/cell counts, child-node names, electrical limit properties, fallback ordering, and example layouts become long-lived contracts in DTS and DTB artifacts. Runtime state belongs to drivers and hardware after probe, including power_supply registration, state-of-charge, voltage/current/temperature measurements, battery health, charge counters, alert thresholds, suspend/resume sampling, and cached gauge registers.

## Risks
Primary risks are Devicetree ABI drift, incomplete compatible coverage, wrong fallback ordering, and resource-shape changes that still compile but break driver probe, power sequencing, clock setup, IRQ delivery, or consumer phandle resolution. Tightening required properties can reject existing board files; loosening them can hide missing supplies, clocks, resets, interrupts, current/voltage limits, PWM cell counts, PTP/PPS GPIOs, or regulator child constraints. This schema rejects unknown top-level properties with `additionalProperties: false`. It has 17 top-level properties and 17 distinct nested property names, so broad edits should be checked against all compatibles, examples, and in-tree DTS users rather than only the filename's nominal device.
