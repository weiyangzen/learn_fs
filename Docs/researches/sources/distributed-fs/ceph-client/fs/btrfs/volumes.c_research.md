# sources/distributed-fs/ceph-client/fs/btrfs/volumes.c

## Purpose
`volumes.c` is the Btrfs volume-management core. It tracks scanned devices by filesystem UUID, opens and closes block devices for mount, loads device/chunk metadata, allocates and removes chunks, implements device add/remove/resize/sprout flows, drives balance relocation, maps logical filesystem addresses to physical device stripes, persists device statistics, and verifies chunk-to-device-extent consistency before writable use.

The file is also the coordination point for several whole-filesystem exclusive operations: balance, device add, device remove, device replace, and resize. It keeps the in-memory device registry, the chunk mapping tree, superblock system chunk array, chunk/dev btree items, and per-device accounting in sync.

## Important APIs, types, and functions
- `btrfs_raid_array[]`, `btrfs_bg_flags_to_raid_index()`, `btrfs_bg_type_to_raid_name()`, `btrfs_nr_parity_stripes()`, and `btrfs_describe_block_groups()` define and describe profile properties used by allocation, mapping, balance, and degraded checks.
- `uuid_mutex`, `fs_uuids`, `alloc_fs_devices()`, `free_fs_devices()`, `device_list_add()`, `btrfs_scan_one_device()`, `btrfs_open_devices()`, and `btrfs_close_devices()` implement global filesystem-device registration and mount-time open/close.
- `btrfs_alloc_device()`, `btrfs_find_device()`, `btrfs_find_device_by_devspec()`, `btrfs_get_dev_args_from_path()`, and `btrfs_put_dev_args_from_path()` provide device lookup and ioctl argument normalization.
- `find_free_dev_extent()`, `btrfs_first_pending_extent()`, `btrfs_find_hole_in_pending_extents()`, `dev_extent_hole_check()`, and `dev_extent_hole_check_zoned()` locate safe device-space holes while accounting for pending chunk allocations and zoned constraints.
- `btrfs_init_new_device()`, `btrfs_rm_device()`, `btrfs_grow_device()`, `btrfs_shrink_device()`, `btrfs_update_device()`, and `btrfs_scratch_superblocks()` implement add/remove/resize and block-device cleanup behavior.
- `btrfs_init_sprout()`, `btrfs_setup_sprout()`, `btrfs_finish_sprout()`, `open_seed_devices()`, and `init_first_rw_device()` handle read-only seed filesystems becoming writable sprouts.
- `btrfs_create_chunk()`, `create_chunk()`, `btrfs_chunk_alloc_add_chunk_item()`, `btrfs_remove_chunk()`, `btrfs_remove_dev_extents()`, and `btrfs_free_chunk()` create and remove logical block groups, chunk items, and device extents.
- `btrfs_add_chunk_map()`, `btrfs_remove_chunk_map()`, `btrfs_find_chunk_map()`, `btrfs_get_chunk_map()`, `btrfs_mapping_tree_free()`, and `btrfs_alloc_chunk_map()` own the in-memory logical-to-physical `struct btrfs_chunk_map` rb-tree.
- `btrfs_balance()`, `__btrfs_balance()`, `btrfs_recover_balance()`, `btrfs_resume_balance_async()`, `btrfs_pause_balance()`, and `btrfs_cancel_balance()` implement persistent balance state, filtering, relocation, pause/resume, and cancellation.
- `btrfs_relocate_chunk()`, `btrfs_relocate_chunk_finish()`, `btrfs_relocate_sys_chunks()`, and `btrfs_may_alloc_data_chunk()` coordinate block-group relocation with chunk removal.
- `btrfs_map_block()`, `btrfs_map_discard()`, `btrfs_map_repair_block()`, `btrfs_num_copies()`, `btrfs_chunk_writeable()`, and `btrfs_full_stripe_len()` are the exported mapping APIs consumed by bio, scrub, RAID56, zoned, and repair paths.
- `btrfs_read_sys_array()`, `btrfs_read_chunk_tree()`, `read_one_dev()`, and `read_one_chunk()` reconstruct mount-time device state and chunk maps from the superblock and chunk tree.
- `btrfs_init_dev_stats()`, `btrfs_run_dev_stats()`, `btrfs_dev_stat_inc_and_print()`, `btrfs_get_dev_stats()`, and `btrfs_commit_device_sizes()` load, update, report, and commit per-device persistent counters and committed size snapshots.
- `btrfs_verify_dev_extents()`, `verify_one_dev_extent()`, `verify_chunk_dev_extent_mapping()`, `btrfs_verify_dev_items()`, and `btrfs_check_rw_degradable()` are defensive mount/writable-mode checks.

## Control flow
Device discovery begins with `btrfs_scan_one_device()` under `uuid_mutex`. It opens a block device read-only, reads the primary superblock with `btrfs_read_disk_super()`, optionally skips registration for simple single-device non-seed scans, and calls `device_list_add()`. `device_list_add()` finds or creates a `struct btrfs_fs_devices` entry keyed by fsid/metadata UUID, handles temp-fsid cases for single-device collisions, creates or updates `struct btrfs_device`, records paths under RCU, and updates generation and total device counts. Mount later calls `btrfs_open_devices()`, which sorts by devid and opens each registered device through `btrfs_open_one_device()`, validates devid/UUID against the disk superblock, sets writable/seeding/discard/rotational flags, populates `bdev`, and builds the writable allocation list.

Mount-time metadata loading is staged. `btrfs_read_sys_array()` maps system chunks embedded in the superblock first so the chunk tree can be accessed. `btrfs_read_chunk_tree()` then scans `DEV_ITEM`s followed by `CHUNK_ITEM`s. `read_one_dev()` resolves normal and seed devices, creates missing placeholders on degraded mounts, fills device geometry/accounting from the dev item, and updates free chunk space for writable devices. `read_one_chunk()` creates a `btrfs_chunk_map`, resolves each stripe to a device or missing placeholder, sets `IN_FS_METADATA`, derives trusted profile parameters, and inserts the map into the mapping tree. Afterward `btrfs_verify_dev_extents()` can walk the device tree to ensure every dev extent has a matching chunk stripe and every chunk stripe was verified.

Chunk allocation starts in callers such as block-group allocation or sprout initialization with `btrfs_create_chunk()` while holding `chunk_mutex`. The function validates the profile, determines the next logical chunk offset, initializes an `alloc_chunk_ctl` from `btrfs_raid_array[]`, gathers writable devices and their largest holes, sorts devices by available space, picks stripe count and stripe size for regular or zoned allocation, and calls `create_chunk()`. `create_chunk()` allocates a `btrfs_chunk_map`, fills stripes, inserts the map, creates the block group, increments device `bytes_used`, queues devices for post-commit size snapshots, decrements `free_chunk_space`, sets needed incompat flags, and refreshes per-profile availability. `btrfs_chunk_alloc_add_chunk_item()` is the persistence phase: it serializes the map into a `CHUNK_ITEM`, updates each device item, inserts into the chunk tree, and mirrors system chunks into the superblock system array.

Chunk removal is relocation-first. `btrfs_relocate_chunk()` requires `reclaim_bgs_lock`, pauses scrub, relocates extents out of the target block group, then either leaves remap-tree relocation to finish separately or calls `btrfs_relocate_chunk_finish()`. The finish path starts a transaction, calls `btrfs_remove_chunk()`, and ends the transaction. `btrfs_remove_chunk()` deletes device extents first, updates device bytes used and free chunk space, then takes `chunk_mutex` to update device items, delete the chunk item, remove a system chunk from the superblock array if needed, release reserved chunk metadata, and finally remove the block group.

Device add/remove/resize are built on those primitives. `btrfs_init_new_device()` opens a new block device for write, validates zone compatibility and size, allocates a `btrfs_device`, starts a transaction, adds it to device and allocation lists under `device_list_mutex` plus `chunk_mutex`, updates superblock totals, creates a sysfs entry, persists a dev item, and commits. If adding to a seed filesystem, it first creates a sprout device set, changes the writable fsid, allocates first metadata/system chunks, persists seed generations, updates sysfs fsid state, and relocates system chunks. `btrfs_rm_device()` checks RAID minimums, active swapfile pins, replace target state, and last-writable-device constraints, removes the device from allocation, shrinks it to zero by relocating all extents past size zero, deletes the dev item, detaches it from lists, scratches its superblocks, and hands the open block-device file to the caller for final release. `btrfs_shrink_device()` temporarily lowers the in-memory size, commits pending allocations if they overlap the shrinking range, relocates every dev extent beyond the new size, then persists the smaller dev item and super total. `btrfs_grow_device()` expands totals, queues post-commit snapshots, clears space-full flags, and updates the dev item.

Balance persists its requested filters in a `BALANCE_OBJECTID/TEMPORARY_ITEM` before relocating. `btrfs_balance()` validates mixed-mode rules, target profiles, device counts, and forced redundancy reduction. It installs `fs_info->balance_ctl`, marks balance running, drops `balance_mutex`, and calls `__btrfs_balance()`. The inner loop first counts expected chunks, then scans backward through chunk items, applies profile/usage/devid/drange/vrange/stripe/soft/limit filters in `should_balance_chunk()`, optionally reserves an empty data chunk to avoid losing the only data profile, and relocates matching chunks. Metadata remap chunks are collected and handled by `balance_remap_chunks()` using the remap tree. Pause and cancel requests are observed between chunks. On normal completion or cancel, `reset_balance_state()` removes the on-disk balance item and clears the exclusive operation; on pause, the item remains so `btrfs_recover_balance()` can reconstruct `balance_ctl` on mount and `btrfs_resume_balance_async()` can restart it.

Logical I/O mapping runs through `btrfs_map_block()`. It finds the containing chunk map, translates remapped block groups if needed, limits length to the chunk and stripe/full-stripe boundary, checks mirror numbers, and reads device-replace state. Profile-specific helpers compute the target stripe set: RAID0 stripes by modulo, RAID1/RAID10 select a live mirror for reads or all mirrors for writes, DUP maps duplicated stripes, RAID5/6 reads direct data stripes and maps writes/recovery to full stripes, and SINGLE maps one stripe. If possible, a single `btrfs_io_stripe` is returned without heap allocation; otherwise a `btrfs_io_context` is allocated and filled. For writes during device replace, `handle_ops_on_dev_replace()` duplicates source-device writes to the target device and adjusts stripe/error tolerance. `btrfs_map_discard()` separately maps logical discard ranges into one or more physical stripes, excluding replace targets and rejecting RAID56. `btrfs_map_repair_block()` maps read-repair or scrub writes to exactly one physical mirror, including RAID56 data-stripe selection from a full-stripe mapping.

## State and persistence behavior
The global `fs_uuids` list persists only in memory and is protected by `uuid_mutex`; each `btrfs_fs_devices` owns device lists, seed lists, allocation lists, total/open/missing/rw counts, per-profile availability, read policy, and allocation policy. Individual `btrfs_device` objects track the path under RCU, block device/file handles, device id and UUID, size and bytes-used pairs, committed size snapshots, zone info, allocation-state extents, dev stats, sysfs kobject state, and status bits such as `WRITEABLE`, `IN_FS_METADATA`, `MISSING`, `REPLACE_TGT`, `FLUSH_FAILED`, and `ITEM_FOUND`.

Persistent state is spread across several on-disk structures. Superblocks contain fsid/metadata UUIDs, total bytes, number of devices, seeding flags, and the compact system chunk array. The chunk tree contains dev items and chunk items. The device tree contains dev extents and persistent dev stats items. Balance state is a temporary item in the tree root. Device add/remove/resize and chunk allocation/removal update these trees in transactions, and device size snapshots are finalized at transaction commit by `btrfs_commit_device_sizes()`.

The mapping tree is an in-memory rb-tree of `btrfs_chunk_map` objects guarded by `mapping_tree_lock` and reference counted. Inserting a map also marks corresponding per-device `alloc_state` extents with `CHUNK_ALLOCATED`; removal clears those bits. Pending allocation state is essential because chunk creation can happen before all on-disk items are visible in the committed device tree, so free-space searches must exclude pending extents to avoid double allocation.

Space accounting is updated eagerly in memory: device `bytes_used`, fs-wide `free_chunk_space`, `total_rw_bytes`, superblock total bytes, and per-profile availability are adjusted under `chunk_mutex`. The corresponding disk state is written through dev items, chunk items, dev extents, and later transaction-commit snapshots. Error paths often restore in-memory totals or abort the transaction if persistence is already partially modified.

Seed/sprout state is special. Seed devices remain read-only in private seed `fs_devices` instances; adding a writable device splices existing devices to a seed list, generates a new fsid for the sprout filesystem, clears the seeding super flag, and records expected seed generations so later mount can validate seed identity.

## Dependencies and integration points
This file depends on the Linux block layer for opening block devices, setting block size, sync/invalidate, discard, zoned reset, device properties, and `bdev` lifetime. It integrates with Btrfs tree accessors, transaction handling, the chunk/tree/device roots, block-group creation/removal, extent relocation, scrub pause/cancel, RAID56 helpers, RAID stripe tree remapping, dev-replace state, zoned allocation helpers, discard control, sysfs device/fSID entries, UUID tree support, ioctl argument formats, mount options, message/logging helpers, and swapfile pin tracking.

Primary callers include mount and disk open code in `disk-io.c`, ioctl device/balance/resize paths in `ioctl.c`, block-group allocation in `block-group.c`, physical I/O submission in `bio.c`, scrub repair and mirror enumeration in `scrub.c`, dev-replace finishing in `dev-replace.c`, zoned code in `zoned.c`, and relocation/remap-tree code in `relocation.c`.

Locking integration is central. `uuid_mutex` protects the global fsid registry and mount/open/close device assembly. `device_list_mutex` protects per-filesystem device list changes and readers that cannot use RCU. `chunk_mutex` serializes chunk tree changes, system chunk array updates, allocation-list accounting, and transaction post-commit device lists. `balance_mutex` and `balance_lock` protect balance lifecycle and status. `reclaim_bgs_lock` prevents races with automatic unused block-group removal around relocation. `dev_replace.rwsem` keeps I/O mapping and replacement finishing coordinated.

## Risks
Important correctness risks are concentrated around consistency between in-memory and on-disk volume state. A chunk allocation bug can double-allocate physical device space if pending extents, committed dev extents, zoned empty-zone checks, or replace-target exclusions are missed. A chunk removal bug can leave dangling chunk maps, stale dev extents, incorrect device `bytes_used`, or missing system chunk array entries. Device add/remove/resize error unwinding must restore allocation lists, counters, super totals, sysfs state, block-device references, and transaction state in the right order.

Lock ordering is high risk because device operations touch block-device opens, tree COW, chunk allocation, dev-replace finishing, sysfs, scrub, and transaction commit. The documented nesting of `uuid_mutex`, `device_list_mutex`, `chunk_mutex`, and `balance_mutex` is part of the API contract. Violating the `device_list_mutex`/`chunk_mutex` ordering around dev replace or taking `chunk_mutex` while deleting dev extents in a path that can COW and allocate metadata can deadlock.

Balance and relocation risks include losing the only data profile, mishandling pause/cancel so the on-disk balance item disagrees with `exclusive_operation`, relocating chunks while automatic block-group deletion races, and not tolerating transient `-ENOSPC` where retry or preallocation is expected. Metadata remap chunks follow a separate path and need careful remap-root COW and block-group read-only handling.

I/O mapping risks include wrong mirror selection, off-by-one stripe math, overlong mapped lengths across stripe/full-stripe boundaries, RAID56 parity rotation mistakes, invalid mirror numbers, stale maps during remap translation, and missing duplication to the device-replace target. Single-stripe optimization must not bypass RAID stripe tree offset translation or RAID56 repair rules.

Mount-time degraded handling is another sensitive area. Missing-device placeholders must be created only when degraded mode allows it, seed-device generations must match, superblock `num_devices` and total bytes must be reconciled carefully, and writable degraded mounts must reject chunk layouts whose missing/flush-failed devices exceed profile tolerance.

## Test signals
Local unit-style coverage exists for several exported helpers: `tests/chunk-allocation-tests.c` exercises `btrfs_find_hole_in_pending_extents()`, while `tests/extent-map-tests.c` and `tests/zoned-tests.c` allocate and insert chunk maps through `btrfs_alloc_chunk_map()` and `btrfs_add_chunk_map()`. These are useful signals for pending extent hole selection, chunk-map rb-tree behavior, and zoned/simple mapping assumptions.

Broader validation should cover mount scanning and assembly with single-device, multi-device, metadata UUID, temp-fsid, seed, sprout, missing-device, and stale-device cases. Device add/remove/resize tests should verify superblock totals, dev items, dev extents, sysfs entries, udev-visible superblock scratching, and rollback after injected failures. Chunk allocation tests should include all profiles, device-count minima, uneven free-space holes, DUP, RAID10, RAID5/6, system chunk limits, per-profile availability, and zoned zone-aligned allocation/reset behavior.

Balance test signals include start, pause, cancel, resume after remount, persisted balance-item recovery, usage/profile/devid/range/limit/soft filters, conversion with and without forced metadata redundancy reduction, `-ENOSPC` retry behavior, active swapfile skips, system chunk relocation, and metadata remap chunks. I/O mapping tests should exercise read and write mapping for SINGLE, DUP, RAID0, RAID1, RAID1C3/4, RAID10, RAID5, RAID6, device replace duplication, remap-tree translation, mirror-number validation, discard mapping rejection for RAID56, repair writes to a selected mirror, and mapped-length truncation at stripe/full-stripe boundaries.

Mount-time verification signals include corrupt or overlapping dev extents, dev extents beyond device size, mismatched dev extent length versus chunk stripe length, missing dev extents for chunk stripes, devices registered but not present in the chunk tree, 32-bit metadata chunk limits, and writable degraded checks with missing or flush-failed devices. Runtime signals include device stat persistence/reset, flush error clearing across unmount/remount, transaction aborts on critical chunk/dev item failures, and lockdep coverage for device-list/chunk/balance paths.
