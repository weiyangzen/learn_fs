# sources/distributed-fs/tahoe-lafs/src/allmydata/test/test_download.py

Purpose: regression-tests Tahoe-LAFS immutable and mutable download behavior across historical share formats, no-network grid failover, segment/range reads, producer cancellation, corruption handling, download status reporting, and segment fetcher share selection. It is a broad integration-heavy test module for the immutable downloader and its surrounding node, share, and consumer contracts.

Important APIs and types include `_Base.create_shares`, `_Base.load_shares`, `download_immutable`, `download_mutable`, `DownloadTest`, `Corruption`, `DownloadV2`, `Status`, `Selection`, `BrokenDecoder`, the pausing/stopping consumer fakes, `MySegmentFetcher`, `MyShare`, and `FakeNode`. The tests exercise `download_to_data`, `ImmutableFileNode.read`, `CHKFileNode.get_segment`, `DownloadStatus`, `SegmentFetcher`, `CRSDecoder`, immutable layout parsing, storage server version flags, and error types such as `NoSharesError`, `NotEnoughSharesError`, `DownloadStopped`, `BadSegmentNumberError`, and `BadCiphertextHashError`.

Control flow starts with embedded old immutable and mutable shares plus URIs, generated by the helper path but normally loaded into the no-network grid by `load_shares`. `DownloadTest` then verifies plain round trips, repeated node reuse, failover after deleting already-used shares, complete failure after removing enough shares, mid-download server loss, long/int offset reads, guessed segment-size retries, simultaneous and sequential ranged reads, duplicate share handling, verify-cap ciphertext reads, and old-server read-overrun fallback. Producer tests use consumers that pause, stop, or stop immediately after registration to prove downloader cleanup and cancellation semantics.

State and persistence are mostly temporary no-network storage-server share directories under each test's basedir. The module writes, deletes, restores, and corrupts share files to force downloader paths, and it manipulates per-client cached share state, the NodeMaker weak cache, storage broker version dictionaries, and global `layout.FORCE_V2` during `DownloadV2`. `DownloadStatus` tests are in-memory status event transitions. `Selection` tests are fully synthetic and drive `SegmentFetcher` by feeding fake shares and block activity states.

Dependencies include Twisted `Deferred`/reactor utilities, Foolscap eventual queue helpers, Tahoe URI/storage layout/hash/span/log utilities, no-network grid fixtures, immutable upload and downloader internals, `MemoryConsumer`, `CRSDecoder`, and test mixins. Integration points are deep: upload creates shares, storage directories expose historical data, downloader share discovery populates internal `_shares`, consumers interact with Twisted producer APIs, and segment fetcher policies decide which servers and share numbers to request.

Risks covered are compatibility with old share encodings, stale guessed metadata, overrun-incompatible storage servers, data corruption in share headers, block hash trees, ciphertext hash trees, share hashes and UEBs, duplicate or redundant shares, simultaneous request progress loss, cancellation after failure, and server diversity choices. Residual risks are that many assertions inspect private attributes and exact internal state strings, so downloader refactors may need test adaptation even when user-facing behavior is preserved. The embedded share blobs are historical fixtures and large enough that accidental edits could silently change compatibility coverage.

Test signals are strong integration signals rather than isolated unit checks: successful plaintext recovery after corruption in one share, expected `NoSharesError`/`NotEnoughSharesError` boundaries, no callbacks after segment cancellation or terminator shutdown, `BadCiphertextHashError` from a deliberately broken decoder, progress/active status accounting, and deterministic `SegmentFetcher` choices for diversity, overdue, dead, duplicate, and no-more-shares cases.

Test signals are exact string comparisons for most object types, regex match for the function location, membership in accepted large-integer abbreviations, and accepted variants for exception representations.
